# Brief: swe-theory — market-making theory for binary settlement markets with jump risk, and a concrete quoting rule for Dota using our 300 s model

Research + design, read-only. Web via curl/python or `agent-browser --session swe-theory`. Cite papers/URLs with dates. Read `$R/00-context.md` and `$R/work/orchestrator/explore/strategy.md` first so the rule fits our setting.

Setting: Polymarket Dota map-winner market, binary tokens, tick 0.01, min order 5 shares, maker fee 0, maker rebate 0.15 × 0.05 × p(1−p) per share filled, taker fee 0.05 × p(1−p) per share; the price is a probability that jumps on kills/objectives (our tapes show 10 s jumps >= 5 ticks regularly in fights) and settles to 0/1 at game end (30-60 min). We have a LightGBM forecast of the 300 s-ahead midpoint change (delta), re-anchored to the live book every 0.25 s, and a kill gate (victim token BUYs blocked up to 10 s after a scoreboard kill). Flow: ~$300-700/min taker notional per map, spread p50 3 cents, best depth ~$40, ~30 other makers.

Questions:

1. **Theory.** Avellaneda-Stoikov (2008) and Guéant-Lehalle-Fernandez-Tapia for inventory risk; Glosten-Milgrom / Kyle for adverse selection; extensions with jumps (Cartea-Jaimungal "Algorithmic and High-Frequency Trading" ch. 10, jump-diffusion MM papers), and with a terminal time where the asset settles (prediction markets: Othman & Sandholm LMSR market makers, "automated market making in prediction markets", any paper on CLOB market making in binary/prediction markets, Polymarket-specific MM write-ups and open-source bots: warproxxx/poly-maker, others on GitHub 2025-2026). Extract what each says about: reservation price with inventory, optimal half-spread vs volatility and arrival intensity, how jump risk changes the spread, how settlement time changes it (inventory has a hard horizon), and what to do when the maker has a short-horizon forecast (our delta).
2. **Buy-both + merge vs sell.** Show formally when "bid YES at b, bid NO at 1 − a" equals "bid b / ask a on YES"; fee treatment on each leg (taker fee is on the aggressor; our legs are maker); capital: a resting BUY NO locks (1 − a) USDC while a resting SELL YES locks shares. Explain why a maker with no inventory can only quote the ask via BUY NO, and why merge (not sell) is the natural exit. Any difference in queue priority or in CLOB matching (mirrored matching of YES bid vs NO bid)?
3. **Edge decomposition.** Expected PnL per round trip = spread captured − adverse selection − inventory cost + rebate. With our numbers (spread 3 cents, our half-spread h, jump distribution from `grok-sim` Part A when it appears in `$R/reports/grok-sim.md`; until then assume 10 s mid change p90 = 2 ticks, p99 = 6 ticks, and jump ≥ 5 ticks about once per 3 minutes in fights), give a back-of-envelope formula and a table of expected PnL per $ for h ∈ {1, 2, 3} ticks with and without a forecast that removes a fraction f of adverse selection. What f does our model need to make h = 1 tick profitable? (Our Follow300 live edge is ~3 cents per $ bought on fills gated by |delta| >= 2 cents; use it as a calibration anchor for the model's information content.)
4. **Concrete rule for Dota.** Write the quoting rule as formulas and a parameter table:
   - fair = mid + k × delta (k ∈ [0,1]), optionally microprice;
   - reservation r = fair − g × net (net in shares; convert g from AS: g = γσ²(T − t));
   - half-spread h(t) = h0 + c_σ × σ_short + c_jump × (recent jump indicator) ; floor 1 tick; widen near settlement when |fair − 0.5| is large? (settlement variance p(1−p) shrinks near 0/1: argue the right band, e.g. quote only 0.05..0.95);
   - sizes per side as a function of net (pull the adding side at |net| > N_max; taker flatten at N_taker — when is paying 0.05 × p(1−p) worth it vs waiting for the maker fill?);
   - merge threshold M and timing (capital recycling: show turnover = volume / capital as a function of M and fill rate);
   - interaction with the kill gate and with the 480 s BUY cutoff (argue whether to lift it and why the trader quotes the whole map).
   Give suggested starting values and the ranges for the experiment-zero grid (`grok-sim` brief uses h ∈ {1,2,3} ticks, g ∈ {0, 2e-4, 5e-4}, N_max ∈ {100, 300}, M = 20, s ∈ {20, 50}); say which to change.
5. **Risks specific to Dota** vs CS2/LoL: 30-60 min maps with long fights, pauses, GRID feed lag 1-10 s, lower flow than CS2 (capacity), the all-or-nothing settlement with leftover inventory; what the trader's "pair_avg_cost > 1 in 30% of markets" implies in this framework (directional residual).
6. **What to measure in the backtest** to validate the theory: list the 8-10 metrics (spread captured per fill, 60 s markout of maker fills, inventory half-life, merge turnover, capital peak, PnL per $ by phase) and the acceptance thresholds you would set before going live.
