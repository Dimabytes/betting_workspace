# Brief: leadlag-sol — do we have any information lead in LoL at an 11 s decision lag?

Hypothesis (context fact 2): the Polymarket LoL mid already reflects a game event 4–5 s after the livestats frame stamp, and our model acts at frame stamp + 11 s (`LOL_SOURCE_LAG_SECONDS`, `src/shared/constants/lol.py`; backtest `src/backtest/signals.py` ~line 499–524; training join in `src/lol/05_prepare_dataset.py` `join_market_rows`). If true, the LoL model is predicting a move the market already made, and whatever edge the backtest shows is momentum/mean-reversion, not information. Dota (GRID 10 s, longer stream delays) would be different. Measure it.

Data: `data/lol/processed/datasets/validation.parquet` and `training.parquet` (`match_id, second, state_ts_us, radiant_nw_adv, radiant_xp_adv, deaths_radiant, deaths_dire, market_p_radiant, signal_market_p_radiant_300s`), `market_seconds.parquet` (per-second mids per map; check its schema), `game_features.parquet` (dense per-second game state), `split.parquet` (dates), league via `src/shared/utils/lol_leagues.py`. Dota equivalents: `data/processed/datasets/` (see `src/shared/constants/dataset.py`, `src/prepare_dataset/prepare_dataset.py`) and the Dota market second caches (`src/market_data/build_market_data.py`). Read schemas first; read with `columns=[...]`.

Do:
1. Event-study lead/lag. Define events as the top 5 % of 10 s changes in `radiant_nw_adv` (and separately in `deaths_*`: kills) within seconds 60..480. Align the per-second mid at offsets −60..+120 s around the frame stamp. Plot/tabulate the mean cumulative mid move in the event direction. Report the offset at which 25 % / 50 % / 75 % of the 120 s move is done. Do it for LoL by month (Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep 2026) and for Dota (one or two months). This is the core number.
2. Granger-style check: regress the 300 s future mid change on (a) current features at stamp+11 s, (b) the mid change over the previous 11 s / 30 s / 60 s. How much of the model's R² / MAE gain survives when the recent mid change is given as a competing regressor? Compare LoL vs Dota.
3. Decompose the published LoL model's delta (score `data/lol/models/research`, follow `load_lol_dataset` in `src/lol/06_train_model.py` — copy the logic, do not import 06): what fraction of |delta| ≥ 0.02 entries occur within 30 s after a large event (chasing) vs in quiet periods (anticipating)? Markout of each group. Per month.
4. Lag sensitivity (offline, no retrain): re-align the features by +0 / +5 / +11 / +20 / +30 s relative to the market and re-score the published model's directional markout. If 11 s is already too late, markout should barely change with a later lag and jump with an earlier one. (Features for an earlier lag are what the dataset holds; shift the market side instead.)
5. Repeat 1 and 4 on Dota to show the contrast.

Deliver: a verdict on whether LoL has an information lead at 11 s, with the half-move offsets and the lag-sensitivity table, per period. If there is no lead, state what the surviving edge is (momentum? mean reversion after overshoot?) and what strategy/model change exploits it (e.g. shorter horizon, mid-change features, different entry rule) — and which experiment proves it.
