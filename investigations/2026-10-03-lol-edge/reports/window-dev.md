# window-dev: the full-map vs ≤540 paradox — label geometry, L1 gradient mass, and the principled fix
Status: FINAL

## Verdict

Full-map training hurts the LoL buy window because the label is **non-stationary in `second`**, not because "more data". Conditional on the features the model sees (nw_adv × mid), early and late rows imply different — often opposite — 300s moves: 42% of populated (nw×mid) cells flip sign between <480s and ≥900s, conflict mass ≈1.6¢. The per-second LoL tape gives the ensemble enough rows to *serve* that late regime (914 `second`-splits, median threshold 1006s — the trees carve up the post-540 map while <480 rows share leaves with 480–900s), so pooled L1 medians drag toward the late labels' ~1.5× bigger moves: the full-map model systematically over-predicts |delta|, adds +12.5% entries that mark out 0.73¢ vs 2.80¢ for shared ones, and loses 0.17–0.22¢ of directional markout. Early stopping on the late-dominated holdout then stops at ~52 trees while the buy window wants ~90–130 — re-picking trees recovers only ~half the loss. Dota tolerates full-map because the identical mechanism is ~2–3× weaker (minute rows → coarser regime resolution, smaller late labels, smaller |moves|) — its w540 run was +$3.9k non-significant *in the same direction*. The right fix is distribution-matching: train on what the strategy acts on (the ≤540 window, optionally softened to an entry-density taper). The sell side is a separate, worse problem: **both** LoL models are directionally wrong past ~1200s (≤540: −1.2…−1.5¢; full-map: −2.5¢), i.e. worse than no-move — late-map direction is unlearnable (reversion), so no second model fixes it; the fix is to stop letting the model steer exits late.

## Findings

1. **Late-map LoL rows are numerous and *large*-magnitude, not low-variance noise** (the brief's alternative is falsified). Labeled validation rows: 40.3% ≤540 / 32.3% <480 — i.e. ~60% of labeled rows (and of L1 gradient mass, which is uniform per row) come from seconds the strategy never buys on. Their mean |300s move| is 14.3¢ at ≥1200s vs 9.2¢ at <480 — **1.56× bigger** (label_geometry.csv). At 1500s+, ~47% of rows sit outside mid 0.10–0.90. Late labels are resolution moves, not flat drift. [verified]
   - Command: `uv run python work/window-dev/label_geometry.py` → label_geometry.csv.

2. **Gradient-mass share is NOT the LoL/Dota differentiator.** Dota training is *more* late-dominated: 34.9% ≤540 / 28.3% <480 of 31,743 labeled minute-rows (LoL: 40.2%/32.2% of 2,739,133). If share alone decided, Dota would suffer more. The differentiator is how much the extra rows can distort the fitted function (see 4–6). [verified]

3. **P(label|features) changes sign between early and late at matched features.** On validation, E[label | nw_adv bin] at +1–2k gold: +3.5¢/+1.6¢ (0–300/300–480) → −0.5¢ (900–1500); at +2–4k: +1.9/+2.5 → −0.6 → −3.0¢ (1500–2400); at −8…−4k: −3.2 → +0.5 → **+6.4¢**. This survives conditioning on `market_p_radiant` — e.g. (0,1k] nw × (0.45,0.55) mid: early +2.40¢ vs late −0.02¢; (0,1k] × (0.65,0.8): +1.84 vs −0.42; (1k,2k] × (0.2,0.35): −2.09 vs +6.33. Min-share-weighted |early−late| = **1.57¢**; 13/31 cells flip sign (conflict_mid.csv). Mechanism: surviving long maps are selected for stalled leads → late labels revert where early labels continue. [verified]

4. **`second` is feature #1 of the catalog** (dota_features.py:99) and the full-map model spends it on the late map: 914 `second`-splits, 1.79% gain, thresholds p25/med/p75 = 923/1006/1467 — only **2.0%** of thresholds <480. The ≤540 LIVE model uses `second` *inside* the buy window: 42 splits, 83% of thresholds <480. So the trees can and do gate the regimes — they just gate the wrong side: buy-window rows get pooled with 480–900 rows whose labels already diverge. [verified — model_structure.py]

5. **Interference measured on the published models** (full-map `20261002T165228Z` vs ≤540 LIVE `20261003T091511Z`, same 840k validation rows <480): |d_full| > |d_540| in *every* nw bin (medians dragged toward late labels' bigger moves), sign flips on 2.5–15.6% of rows per bin (max in the near-even −1k..+1k cells), DIR 1.235 vs 1.401¢ = **−0.167¢** (diagnostic fits measured −0.22¢). Entries 271k vs 241k (+12.5%); full-map-only entries mark out **0.73¢** vs 2.80¢ shared / 1.79¢ w540-only; overall entry markout 2.31 vs 2.67¢. This is exactly the backtest signature (more fills, worse ¢/share). [verified — interference_and_sell.py, predictions.parquet]

6. **Early stopping is ~half the story, not all of it.** training_curves.csv (prior investigation, reproduced here): `_full` members reach full-map L1 minimum at iterations 31–63, while buy-window L1 keeps improving to 76–130; `_early` members' minima coincide (~38–48). Re-picking trees by buy-window L1 recovers DIR 1.168→1.269 of 1.389 — the residual −0.12¢ is the leaf-median drag from #3/#5, not the stop count. [verified]

7. **Diagnostic fits (3 members, ≤300k rows, allowed) isolate distribution vs volume.** Variant (d): same 300k budget but at the real 40/60 full-map proportions → DIR −0.11¢, entries +14%, entry markout −0.24¢ vs the ≤540 control — and it grew *more* trees (76–81 vs ~53), so the damage is not "too few trees". Variant (b) inverse-std weights: null (label std is flat 0.064–0.094 inside ≤540). Variant (c) entry-density weights (histfix fills/row: ~0.0003 at 0–60s, ~0.05 at 120–480s): DIR +0.04 (all) / **+0.09¢ (backtest maps)**, entry markout +0.06/0.09¢ — small positive hint, the smooth version of the hard window. [verified — diagnostic_fits.py]

8. **Dota contrast, same measurement:** LIVE full-map (`20261001T220726Z`, 69 trees) vs capped w540 model (`dota-train-540/model`, 46 trees) on the same capped-validation rows <480: DIR 1.754 vs 1.832¢ → full-map costs **−0.078¢** (LoL: −0.167 on the identical test), mae_gain actually favors full-map (0.291 vs 0.279¢), entries 93.3k@3.58 vs 91.6k@3.78¢. Same direction as LoL, ~2–3× smaller — consistent with the published backtest (+$3.9k, p>0.3, for w540). The mechanism is real for Dota too, just below the PnL threshold: 33k minute rows → coarse leaves (~10 maps/leaf) that can't carve the late regime, late labels only 1.44× bigger, |moves| 1.2× smaller per flip. [verified — dota_interference.py]

9. **Sell side: the model is out of its support past ~1200s — and a full-map model is worse there, not better.** ≤540 LIVE on validation 540–2400: mae_gain +0.28¢ at 540–600 decaying through +0.18 at 900–1200 to **−0.002…−0.097 past 1200** (worse than predicting no-move); DIR +1.0¢ at 540–600 → −1.25¢ (1200–1500) → −1.54¢ (1800–2100). The full-map model, which *trained* on those rows: mae_gain better (+0.33¢ overall) but DIR **even more negative** late (−1.0 at 1200–1500, **−2.48¢** at 2100s) — bigger predicted deltas → more confident wrong-way calls. Backtest evidence agrees: w540lv6 >540s sell fills mark out **−0.413¢/share** (lol-exit-model.md) and the trained exit model moved that only +0.03¢. Mechanism: `_choose_sell_target` rests the exit at max(join_ask, fair) (quoting.py:467–479), so a wrong-direction late fair parks the sell above the book and delays/warps the exit; when the delta collapses the order ratchets down to the ask and fills into a dip that recovers (+0.41¢ over 300s). [verified]

10. **What the L1 + early-stopper actually optimizes.** `LGB_PARAMS` = regression_l1, lr 0.05, 63 leaves, min_data 200 (gbm.py:27–34). Under L1 every row carries unit gradient magnitude: the fit is a *row-share*-weighted median pursuit. On the current ≤540 recipe, early stopping sees ~1.05M rows ≤540 (06_train_model.py:183–195: `load_lol_dataset` → `slice_model_rows` keeps `second ≤ LOL_TRAIN_END_SECOND = 540`, constants.py:126) while reported metrics use <480 (06_train_model.py:206, `BUY_CUTOFF_SECOND`). Under full-map it saw 2.6M rows — 68% of them off-distribution for buying. Dota differs structurally: `slice_train_rows` (train_model.py:122–125) keeps ~18 **minute** rows/map vs LoL's ~1,010 labeled rows/map; its fit-validation is also full-map per-second (train_model.py:116–119), so Dota's early-stop is late-dominated too — but its coarse model can't exploit it. [verified — code + counts]

## What I ruled out

- **"Late rows are low-variance/near-bounds filler"**: wrong — late LoL labels are *bigger* moves (1.56×) and extreme-mid rows are only ~47% even at 1500s+.
- **"It's just the row-share of late data"**: Dota is more late-dominated (28% <480) and fine.
- **"It's just the tree count"**: buy-window re-selection recovers ≈half the DIR loss; diagnostic (d) with *more* trees still lost DIR.
- **"Mid/logit already separates the regimes"**: the early-vs-late conflict persists inside matched (nw×mid) cells — 42% sign flips.
- **"A second full-map/exit model fixes the sell side"**: tested negative (exit-full-l1 −$1,633 coin-flip, l2 −$8,782), and the full-map model is itself *more* wrong-direction past 1200s (−2.5¢ at 2100s). The bottleneck is exit mechanics, not fair value.
- **Truncation artifacts**: frac_labeled declines smoothly with map-end approach (0.93 → 0.22 by 2340s); nothing pathological in the label coverage per bucket.

## Proposed experiments

Ranked by expected value; every one judged on the **late chronological third** plus the whole run.

1. **Entry-density tapered weights — the smooth, principled window** (replaces or refines the hard 540 cut).
   Trainer change: in `slice_model_rows` (src/lol/06_train_model.py:81–87) keep every labeled row but emit a `w` column: `w = fills_per_row(bucket)` from `work/window-dev/entry_seconds.csv` (or the analytic equivalent: ~1.0 on 60–540, ~0.05 on 0–60, tapering to ~0.02 at 900–1500, 0 beyond), normalized to mean 1; pass `weight=frame["w"]` in `fit_ensemble_member_booster` (src/shared/utils/gbm.py:164, `lgb.Dataset(train_X, train_y, weight=w)`) and through `ResearchMemberFit.__call__` (gbm.py:239–246). Keep early-stop set = ≤540 (or <480 — see #2). My 3-member hint on ≤540 alone: DIR +0.04…+0.09¢, entry markout +0.06…+0.09¢.
   Command after code change: standard `make lol-train` then `scripts/compare_backtests.py` vs `validation_join_delta02_x015_cut480_p45_histfix-20261003r2`. Watch: DIR & entry-conditioned markout on backtest maps <480, buy fills count (expect −5…10% vs histfix), **late-third net PnL**. Cost: ~10-line diff + 1 retrain + 1 backtest.
2. **Early-stop on the decision window.** Fit-validation = labeled rows <480 (not ≤540, and emphatically not full-map): change the `valid_features`/`build_price_delta_labels` input in `train_research` (06_train_model.py:189–195) to `validation_dataset.loc[validation_dataset["second"] < BUY_CUTOFF_SECOND]`. Evidence: `_full` fits minimized buy-window L1 at 76–130 iterations vs the chosen 50–63; under ≤540-train the minima coincide (~40–50) so expected gain is small but the criterion becomes "stop where you trade" — free insurance the next time the window moves. Watch: member_trees (expect ~equal or +), DIR, late-third. Cost: 3-line diff + retrain.
3. **Exit-side fair decoupling past T\* (policy change, NOT a new model).** For `second > T*` (T\* ≈ 900–1200, where my sell table crosses into negative dir/mae_gain), zero the model's delta contribution to the exit fair (`fair = mid`, i.e. sell at join_ask — stop parking the exit above the book on a directionally-wrong fair). Code points: fair computed in `src/backtest/signals.py`/`calculate_fair_radiant` (signals.py:845) and the live twin in the trader signal path; the sell target itself is `_choose_sell_target` (src/strategy/quoting.py:467–479). A/B in the backtest runner behind a flag — e.g. `--exit-fair-clip-second 1080`. This differs from `lol-exit-model.md`, which swapped in a *better* fair model and kept the mechanics — my data shows that model is itself more wrong-direction late; here we remove model trust where it is provably negative. Expected: bounded upside ~0.4¢/share on the ~540k late-sold shares ≈ **+$2k/seed**; risk: slower exits on genuinely collapsing maps (hold-till-settlement tail). Watch: >540s sell markout (−0.41¢ baseline), late-third PnL, worst-map/CVaR5 (a hold-longer change can fatten the left tail). Cost: small diff + 1 backtest.
   - Do NOT re-propose: full-map training for exit coverage (negative), L2 objective for exits (negative), an unwind clock (already removed & kept removed).
